UNIT 1: THEORY OF CONSUMER BEHAVIOR AND DEMAND

1.1 CARDINAL UTILITY APPROACH
- Utility: Refers to the level of satisfaction or happiness that consumers get from the consumption of goods and services.
- Cardinal Utility Theory: Assumes that utility is measurable in absolute or numerical numbers called "utils".
- Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility: States that as the amount of a good consumed increases, the marginal utility of that good decreases.
- Consumer Optimum Condition: The marginal utility per unit of money spent is equal for all commodities, and total expenditure equals total income.

1.2 ORDINAL UTILITY THEORY AND PREFERENCES
- Ordinal Utility Approach: Assumes utility cannot be measured absolutely; instead, consumers can rank different consumption bundles according to their preferences.
- Indifference Curve (IC): A locus of points showing alternative combinations of two goods that yield the same level of utility or satisfaction to the consumer.
- Properties of Standard Indifference Curves:
  - Downward sloping to the right (negative slope).
  - Do not intersect each other.
  - Higher indifference curves represent higher levels of utility.
  - Convex to the origin due to a diminishing Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS).
- Special Types of Indifference Curves:
  - Perfect Substitutes: Straight, downward-sloping lines representing a constant rate of exchange.
  - Perfect Complements: Right-angled (L-shaped) lines representing goods consumed jointly in fixed proportions.

1.3 THE BUDGET LINE
- Budget Line: A graph indicating the different combinations of two goods that a consumer can purchase with a given income at given market prices.
- Budget Equation: P_X X + P_Y Y = M, where P_X and P_Y are prices, X and Y are quantities, and M is income.
- Slope of the Budget Line: Equal to the negative of the price ratio of the two goods (-P_X / P_Y).
- Shifts and Rotations: Income changes cause parallel shifts in the budget line, while price changes cause the budget line to rotate along the respective axis.

1.4 OPTIMUM OF THE CONSUMER
- Consumer Optimum: The point where the consumer maximizes utility by attaining the highest possible indifference curve given their budget constraint.
- Optimality Conditions: The budget equation is fully spent and the slope of the indifference curve (MRS) equals the slope of the budget line (P_X / P_Y).
- Price Consumption Curve (PCC): The locus of utility-maximizing combinations resulting from variations in the price of one commodity, used to derive the individual demand curve.
- Income Consumption Curve (ICC): The locus of optimal combinations purchased when the consumer's income changes, helping categorize normal and inferior goods.
