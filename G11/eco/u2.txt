UNIT 2: MARKET STRUCTURE AND THE DECISION OF FIRMS

2.1 REVIEW OF MARKET STRUCTURES
- Market: Where consumers and producers interact to determine prices for goods and services.
- Spectrum of Market Competitiveness (from most to least competitive):
  1. Perfect Competition
  2. Monopolistic Competition
  3. Oligopoly
  4. Pure Monopoly

2.2 PERFECT COMPETITION MARKET
Characteristics and Firm Behavior:
- Individual firms are insignificant compared to the market size and supply homogeneous products.
- Prices are determined by market demand and supply forces, making individual firms price-takers and the industry the price-maker.
- The firm's demand curve is horizontal and perfectly elastic, where P = AR = MR.

Equilibrium and Profit Scenarios:
- Short-Run Equilibrium Conditions: Marginal Revenue equals Marginal Cost (MR = MC), and the MC curve must be rising (cutting MR from below).
- Shutdown Point: The intersection of the average variable cost (AVC) curve and the marginal cost curve; firms shut down immediately if the price falls below AVC.
- Long-Run Equilibrium: Firms earn normal profits by adjusting their plant size to produce at the minimum point of the long-run average cost (LAC) curve.

2.3 PURE MONOPOLY MARKET
Characteristics and Curves:
- Single firm with no rivals, no close substitutes, and prohibitively difficult entry barriers.
- The monopolist is a price setter that determines its own price based on output decisions.
- The demand curve is downward-sloping, and the marginal revenue (MR) curve lies beneath it, decreasing twice as fast as demand.

Price Discrimination:
- First-Degree (Primary): Charging each consumer their absolute maximum reservation price, capturing all consumer surplus.
- Second-Degree (Nonlinear): Pricing based on the number of units bought, utilizing quantity discounts or premium pricing.
- Third-Degree (Market Segmentation): Charging different prices to different consumer groups by dividing the market based on varying demand elasticities.

2.4 MONOPOLISTIC COMPETITION MARKET
Characteristics:
- Features many firms selling distinctive, differentiated products, giving them some control over price.
- Product differentiation can be real or spurious (fancied) through physical aspects, location, intangible aspects, or consumer perceptions.
- Short-run profit maximization follows the rule where MR = MC, allowing firms to earn economic profits, break even, or incur losses.

2.5 OLIGOPOLY MARKET
Characteristics and Types:
- Characterized by a few dominant sellers and strategic interdependence, where each firm considers its rivals' reactions when setting policies.
- Can be pure (homogeneous products like steel and cement) or impure/differentiated (differentiated products like automobiles and beverages).
- Collusive Oligopoly: Competing firms collude to minimize competition and maximize joint profit through formal cartels or informal price leadership.
- Non-Collusive Oligopoly: Firms compete independently, illustrated by models like the Cournot model (competition on output) and the Bertrand model (competition on price).
