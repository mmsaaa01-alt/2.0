UNIT 7: MAIN SECTORS, SECTORIAL POLICIES AND STRATEGIES OF ETHIOPIA

7.1 OVERVIEW OF AGRICULTURAL POLICIES AND STRATEGIES
- Uni-modal Agricultural Strategy: Focuses on agricultural transformation through the intensification of small-scale peasant farms, diffusing divisible innovations, and prioritizing food crops to reduce rural poverty.
- Bi-modal Agricultural Strategy: Advocates for a dualistic structure combining small peasant farm intensification with commercialization, allowing entrepreneurial individuals to accumulate land and attract large-scale capital.

7.2 HISTORICAL EVOLUTION OF ETHIOPIA'S AGRICULTURAL POLICIES
- Pre-1974: Focused primarily on industry and infrastructure, later introducing package projects (Comprehensive and Minimum Package Programs) and Large-Scale Mechanized Commercial Farms (LSMCF).
- Derg Regime (1974-1991): Shifted to a centrally planned economy characterized by nationalization, involuntary collectivization, forced villagization, and state control of output markets.
- Post-1991: Liberalized markets and introduced Agricultural Development-Led Industrialization (ADLI), followed by poverty reduction strategies like SDPRP, PASDEP, and recent home-grown economic reforms.

7.3 PROBLEMS AND REMEDIES OF THE AGRICULTURAL SECTOR
- Problems: Natural challenges like recurrent drought and rainfall variability, alongside human-made issues such as land fragmentation, infrastructure shortages, land degradation, and traditional practices.
- Remedies: Expanding irrigation and water harvesting, promoting environmental management and afforestation, crop diversification, strengthening land ownership rights, and improving rural infrastructure.

7.4 INDUSTRIAL SECTOR POLICIES, STRATEGIES, AND CHALLENGES
- Imperial Regime: Focused on import-substituting light industries relying on foreign investment.
- Derg Regime: Nationalized medium-large scale manufacturing enterprises and heavily restricted private investment.
- Post-1991 & Industrial Development Strategy (IDS): Adopted an export-led industrialization strategy focusing on labor-intensive sectors, supported by the establishment of state-of-the-art industrial parks along economic corridors.
- Industrial Problems and Remedies: Hindered by a lack of finance, weak domestic demand, foreign exchange shortages for imported inputs, and technological gaps. Remedies include building macroeconomic stability, improving the ease of doing business, and developing reliable infrastructure.

7.5 SERVICE SECTOR POLICIES AND STRATEGIES IN ETHIOPIA
- Education Sector: Addressed through the Education Sector Development Programme (ESDP) focusing on primary expansion, technical-vocational training, and quality improvements.
- Health Sector: Guided by the 1993 National Health Policy and Health Sector Development Plans (HSDP), emphasizing decentralization, preventive care, and combatting communicable diseases.
- Transport & Tourism: Experienced major deregulation in transport following 1992 policies, while the 2009 Tourism Development Policy aims to optimize returns from Ethiopia's historical, natural, and cultural attractions.
