UNIT THREE: PEOPLES, STATES AND HISTORICAL PROCESSES IN ETHIOPIA AND THE HORN TO THE END OF THE 13TH CENTURY

3.1. LANGUAGES, RELIGIONS AND PEOPLES OF ETHIOPIA AND THE HORN
Language Families:
- Afro-Asiatic Super-family: The most geographically widespread in the region, including three main families in Ethiopia:
  - Cushitic Family: Spoken by the majority. Divided into North (Beja), Central/Agaw (Bilen, Qimant), East (Highland East like Hadiya/Sidama; Lowland East like Afan Oromo, Afar, Somali), and Southern (Tanzania).
  - Omotic Family: Spoken mainly in the Omo Valley (Wolaita, Gamo, Kafa, Dorze, etc.); spoken exclusively in Ethiopia.
  - Semitic Family (Ethio-Semitic): Split into North Ethio-Semitic (Tigre, Tigrigna, Ge'ez) and South Ethio-Semitic (Amharic, Gurage, Argoba, Harari, Silti).
- Nilo-Saharan Super-family: Spoken in Benishangul-Gumuz and Gambella (Anuak, Nuer, Berta, Gumuz, etc.).

Religions:
- Indigenous Religions: Monotheistic belief in a supreme being through spirits. 
  - Waqefanna (Oromo): Worships Waqa (the creator) via Ayyana (divinities). Led by Qallu/Qalliti and Abba Muda (spiritual leader).
  - Hadiya Religion: Supreme being Waa (sun and moon as eyes), with practices like Fandanano.
- Judaism: Entered Ethiopia around the 8th century B.C., linked to the Solomonic legend (Kibre Negest) of Menelik I.
- Christianity: Introduced in the 4th century A.D. King Ezana converted via Frumentius (first bishop consecrated by Patriarch Athanasius). The Nine Saints spread Christianity and translated the Bible into Ge'ez. Saint Yared created church liturgy/music.
- Islam: Began with the First Hijira in 615 A.D. when Prophet Muhammad sent followers to Aksum (welcomed by the king). Entered peacefully and spread via the Dahlak Islands and Zeila routes.

Settlement Patterns:
- Cushitic and Semitic peoples settled between the Red Sea and Blue Nile. Omotic peoples inhabited south-western Ethiopia, while Nilotes settled along the Ethio-Sudanese border.

3.2. PRE-AKSUMITE STATES AND THEIR GEOGRAPHICAL SETTING
- Punt: Earliest known state in the Horn, famous as a trading partner with ancient Egypt (expeditions sent by Pharaoh Sahure and Queen Hatshepsut for myrrh, gold, ebony, and ivory).
- Da'amat (980–400 B.C.): First historically known state in Ethiopia, with its capital at Yeha. It traded gold, silver, and ivory with South Arabian merchants and made iron tools.

---

3.3. THE AKSUMITE KINGDOM
- Rise & Expansion: Formed around 200–100 B.C., expanding from the Red Sea coast to the Nile Valley.
- Economy & Trade: Centered around the port of Adulis, trading ivory, gold, and spices for Mediterranean and Asian goods. Minted gold, silver, and bronze coins from the 3rd to 7th centuries. Ranked alongside Rome, Persia, and China as a global power.
- Achievements: Erected monumental stelae, developed the Ge'ez alphabet and Ethiopic calendar, and engaged in overseas conflicts (King Kaleb in Himyar).
- Decline: Triggered by internal environmental degradation/plagues and external factors like Arab control of trade, destruction of Adulis, and rebellions (Beja, Agaw, Queen Yodit).

---

3.4. THE SULTANATE OF SHEWA
- Foundation: Established around 896 A.D. by Khalid ibn al-Walid, ruled by the Makhzumite dynasty with its capital at Walele.
- Fall: Weakened by conflicts with Damot and Ifat, it was overthrown in 1285 A.D. by the Walasma dynasty of Ifat.

---

3.5. THE ZAGWE DYNASTY
- Foundation: Founded in the mid-12th century by Mera Tekle Haymanot (an Agaw general who overthrew the last Aksumite king).
- Capital: Centered in Lasta at Adafa near Roha (Lalibela).
- Achievements: Famous for constructing rock-hewn monolithic churches (like Bete Giyorgis) as part of a "Second Jerusalem" project.
- End: Overthrown in 1270 A.D. by Yekuno-Amlak, who restored the Solomonic dynasty.

---

3.6. THE KINGDOM OF DAMOT
- Location: A powerful medieval kingdom south of the Abay (Blue Nile) River.
- Relations: Dominated neighboring sultanates and kingdoms until subdued by the Solomonic dynasty in the 14th century, later experiencing north-of-Abay migrations due to Oromo expansion.

---

3.7. THE BETE-ISRAEL (ETHIOPIAN JEWS)
- Location & Status: Lived in Wogera, Quara, Simen, etc., working in traditional handicrafts like pottery and blacksmithing.
- Conflict: Faced intense pressure and revolts against the Christian Kingdom. King Yishaq issued a decree in 1413 labeling unbaptized landholders as "Felasi" (exiles).
- Suppression: Maintained independence through rebellions until their autonomy was finally crushed under King Suseneyos.
