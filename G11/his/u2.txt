UNIT TWO: MAJOR SPOTS OF ANCIENT WORLD CIVILIZATIONS UP TO C.500 A.D

2.1. ANCIENT CIVILIZATIONS OF AFRICA
Ancient Egypt:
- "The Gift of the Nile": Annual floods from the Ethiopian highlands supplied fertile soil and water, while the river served as a natural trade route and political highway.
- Unification: Upper and Lower Egypt were united around 3100 B.C. by King Menes, who established the first capital at Memphis.
- Historical Periods: Divided into the Old Kingdom, Middle Kingdom, and New Kingdom (spanning 31 indigenous dynasties) before falling to Persian, Macedonian-Greek, and Roman rule.
- Government & Society: Pharaohs held absolute power as living gods, assisted by a structured bureaucracy led by a vizier and 42 provincial governors (nomarchs). Society was hierarchical, topped by the pharaoh, nobles, priests, scribes, artisans, and peasants/slaves.
- Women's Status: Enjoyed high legal independence (inheriting property, running businesses, going to court) and worked as doctors, merchants, or priestesses.
- Achievements: Developed polytheistic religions (Amon-Re, Osiris, Isis), monumental pyramids/necropolises, hieroglyphics, medicine, mathematics, and a calendar.

Kush-Meroe:
- Geography & Culture: Flourished in Nubia (south of Egypt to present-day Sudan); strongly influenced by Egyptian art, religion, architecture, and hieroglyphics.
- Napata Period: King Piankhi conquered Egypt in 751 B.C., establishing Egypt's 25th dynasty with its capital at Napata.
- Meroe Capital: Shifted to Meroe around 590 B.C. due to its strategic location on trade routes and access to iron ore.
- Iron Age & Economy: Developed advanced iron-smelting technology and exported gold, ivory, animal skins, and slaves.
- Decline: Reached a golden age before being invaded and sacked by the Aksumite king Ezana in the mid-4th century A.D.

2.2. CIVILIZATIONS IN ASIA
Mesopotamia:
- Geography & Cities: Located "between rivers" (Tigris and Euphrates) in Sumeria (southern Iraq), featuring independent city-states ruled by absolute kings called lugals.
- Achievements: Invented the plough, advanced bronze metalworking, astronomy, mathematics (base-60 system), and cuneiform writing on clay tablets.
- Empires: 
  - Babylonia: Famous for King Hammurabi’s harsh and discriminatory legal code.
  - Assyrian Empire: Highly militarized "Romans of Asia" centered in Ashur and Nineveh.
  - New Babylonian Empire: Glorified under Nebuchadnezzar II with massive walls, the Ishtar Gate, and the Hanging Gardens.
  - Persia: Founded by Cyrus the Great; divided into satrapies using a gold currency (darics) and practicing Zoroastrianism (good vs. evil).

India:
- Indus Valley: Centered around Mohenjo-daro and Harappa (~2500–1500 B.C.) by Dravidians, featuring advanced urban planning, grid street patterns, and drainage systems.
- Indo-Aryans: Migrated into northern India after the Indus civilization's collapse.
- Religions: Hinduism (cycle of rebirth and salvation) and Buddhism (founded by Siddhartha Gautama, teaching the "middle way").
- Scientific Contributions: Progress in surgery, herbal medicine, Arabic numerals, the decimal system, and the invention of zero.

China:
- Dynasties: Evolved from the Shang dynasty (bronze vessels, writing) through the Zhou dynasty to the Qin empire under Qin Shi Huang.
- Philosophy: Confucius (emphasizing moral character, secular harmony, and the Five Cardinal Relationships) and Legalism (strict laws and state authority).
- Inventions: Coal, porcelain, the wheelbarrow, varnish, silk, paper, printing, and gunpowder.

2.3. CIVILIZATIONS IN EUROPE
Ancient Greece:
- City-States (Polis): Flourished as independent city-states like Athens (known for direct democracy and a strong navy) and Sparta.
- Culture & Philosophy: Pioneered philosophy (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle), science, medicine (Hippocrates), drama, and history (Herodotus).
- Hellenistic Age: Following the conquests of Philip II and Alexander the Great, Greek culture blended with Asian and African traditions.

Ancient Rome:
- Republic to Empire: Founded around 753 B.C., transitioning from Etruscan rule to a republic governed by a Senate and patrician-plebeian social classes.
- Expansion: Won the Punic Wars against Carthage, eventually dominating the Mediterranean.
- Pax Romana: Began under Augustus in 27 B.C., marking a 200-year golden age of stability, trade, and engineering marvels (roads, aqueducts, the Colosseum).
- Division & Fall: Split into the West Roman Empire (which collapsed in 476 A.D. after Germanic invasions) and the East Roman/Byzantine Empire (which survived until 1453 A.D.).

2.4. CIVILIZATIONS IN LATIN AMERICA
The Maya:
- Classic Period: Thrived in Mesoamerica (Guatemala, Yucatan Peninsula) through independent city-states like Tikal and Calakmul.
- Agriculture & Culture: Used forest clearing and raised field farming; developed a complex polytheistic religion, a hieroglyphic writing system, a 365-day solar calendar, and a numbering system featuring zero.

The Aztecs:
- Capital & Empire: Established their island capital at Tenochtitlan in 1325 A.D., building a vast tribute empire under rulers like Montezuma II.
- Innovations: Built causeways, chinampas (floating gardens), roads, and advanced medical therapies, alongside practicing human sacrifice to appease their gods.

The Incas:
- Andean Empire: Stretched along the Andes mountains from Ecuador to Chile, governed by an absolute emperor believed to be the son of the sun god, Inti.
- Achievements: Mastered precise stone masonry without cement, built extensive mountain roads and agricultural terraces, performed successful brain surgery, and used quinine for malaria.

2.5. THE RISE AND SPREAD OF CHRISTIANITY
Emergence:
- Origins: Arose from Judaism in Roman-controlled Judea based on the teachings of Jesus of Nazareth, emphasizing love, forgiveness, and monotheism.
- Crucifixion: Jesus was crucified around 30 A.D. under Roman authority, but his followers spread the faith to both Jews and Gentiles.

Organization & Growth:
- Hierarchy: Developed a structured church hierarchy led by bishops, archbishops, patriarchs (Rome, Alexandria, Antioch, Constantinople, Jerusalem), and the Pope.
- Legalization: Faced severe Roman persecution before Emperor Constantine issued the Edict of Milan (313 A.D.), guaranteeing freedom of worship.
- Official Religion: Emperor Theodosius I later declared Christianity the official religion of the Roman Empire.
- Great Schism: The Church split in 1054 A.D. into the Catholic West (Rome) and the Orthodox East (Constantinople).
