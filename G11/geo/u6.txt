UNIT SIX: MAJOR GLOBAL ENVIRONMENTAL CHANGES

6.1 PERSISTENT ENVIRONMENTAL CHANGES
Habitat Destruction:
- Habitat destruction is the alteration of physical environmental factors allowing a species to survive and reproduce, and it serves as a primary driver of biodiversity loss.
- Humans alter landscapes spatially through five distinct methods: perforation, dissection, fragmentation, shrinkage, and attrition.
- Long-term population losses can result from habitat degradation, and extinction debt refers to the time it takes for a species to become extinct following landscape change.

Agricultural Intensification:
- Agricultural intensification increases agricultural output per unit of input through improved seeds, increased fertilizers and pesticides, heavy machinery, and irrigation.
- While boosting productivity, it has contributed to carbon stock reduction, soil degradation (including salinization and compaction), monocropping biodiversity loss, and pesticide pollution.
- Sustainable solutions include improved fertilizer and water use efficiency, integrated pest management, and agroforestry.

Overexploitation of Resources:
- Overexploitation occurs when natural resources are extracted at an excessive rate without regard for regeneration, leading to stock depletion and ecological extinction.
- Specific forms include overfishing, overgrazing, over-logging, and over-drafting aquifers (such as the Ogallala aquifer).
- Solutions include sustainable manufacturing practices, consumer education on recycling, and shifting to alternative renewable energy sources like solar and wind power.

Invasive Alien Species:
- Invasive alien species are non-native organisms that infiltrate ecosystems beyond their historic range, exacerbated by climate change and landscape disturbances.
- They are classified based on their environment into terrestrial and aquatic invasive species (including floating weeds, emergent species like the Water Hyacinth, and submerged weeds).
- Management costs increase significantly the longer action is delayed, moving from low-cost prevention to long-term population control.

6.2 POVERTY - ENVIRONMENT NEXUS
The Vicious Cycle:
- The poverty-environment nexus involves mutually reinforcing relationships where poverty forces people to overexploit natural resources, while environmental degradation deepens poverty.
- This dynamic, downward spiral is context-specific, reflecting geographic location, scale, and socioeconomic characteristics.
- Poverty-environment mainstreaming aims to balance environmental preservation and poverty reduction.

Land Degradation and Deforestation:
- Land degradation is a persistent decline in land conditions involving soil, biological, vegetation, and water resource degradation, linked closely with climate change.
- Deforestation can both destroy and create assets for poor people, but its impact on access to forest goods and services is generally negative.
- A balanced forest strategy involves harnessing forests for poverty reduction, integrating forests into sustainable economic development, and protecting local and global environmental values.

6.3 ENVIRONMENTAL DEGRADATION AND SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT
Causes and Effects:
- Causes of environmental degradation include land disturbance, pollution (air, water, land, noise), rapid overpopulation, landfills, deforestation, illegal dumping, and natural causes.
- Effects include respiratory health problems, loss of biodiversity, ozone layer depletion, disruption of atmospheric processes, natural resource scarcity, and long-term food shortages and famine.

Environmental Sustainability and Adaptation:
- Environmental sustainability refers to maintaining essential ecological processes, preserving genetic diversity, and ensuring sustainable utilization of ecosystems.
- Environmental protection measures include stopping deforestation, government regulations, controlling illegal dumping, recycling, and environmental education.
- Adaptive mechanisms for sustainable production include cultivating diverse crop varieties, utilizing diverse cropping systems, and practicing integrated management of crops and livestock.
