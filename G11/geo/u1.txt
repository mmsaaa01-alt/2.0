UNIT ONE: FORMATION OF THE CONTINENTS

1. FORMATION OF EARTH AND CONTINENTS
Origin of Earth and the Solar System:
- The Big Bang Theory: The most widely supported scientific theory for the origin of the universe, which began roughly 10-20 billion years ago from an abrupt cosmic explosion of high-density, high-temperature matter.
- Earth's Creation: Formed approximately 4.5 billion years ago from a mixture of gas and dust particles orbiting the Sun.

Evolution and Break-up of Continents:
- Rodinia & Pangaea: Initial landmasses coalesced into Rodinia around 750 Ma, leading up to the supercontinent Pangaea between 255 and 210 Ma, which was surrounded by the single water body Panthalasu.
- Continental Drift: Triggered by sea-floor spreading at mid-oceanic ridges, Pangaea broke apart during the Triassic period (210–180 Ma) into Gondwanaland and Laurasia.
- Ongoing Effects: The Pacific Ocean is narrowing, the Atlantic Ocean is widening, the Mediterranean Sea is narrowing, the Himalayas are rising, and Australia is projected to reach the equator in ~60 million years.

2. GEOLOGICAL TIMESCALE AND DATING METHODS
Meaning and Divisions:
- Geological Timescale: A timeframe showing the estimated age of Earth and its life-forms, divided hierarchically into Eons (longest), Eras, Periods, and Epochs.
- Major Eons: Hadean, Archean, Proterozoic, and Phanerozoic. The Precambrian (Hadean, Archean, and Proterozoic) covers about 85% of Earth's history.
- Phanerozoic Eras: Subdivided into Paleozoic ("age of ancient life"), Mesozoic ("age of dinosaurs"), and Cenozoic ("age of mammals").

Dating Methods:
- Relative Age: Focuses on the sequence of events using the Principle of Original Horizontality, Principle of Superposition, and Principle of Cross-Cutting Relationships.
- Absolute Age: Numerical age determined via the radioactive decay of unstable parent isotopes into stable daughter elements (e.g., Carbon-14, Uranium-235, Potassium-40) measured through half-lives.

3. PRESENT-DAY CONTINENTS AND OCEANS
Global Distribution:
- Hydrosphere: Covers over 71% of Earth's surface, with over 97% contained in oceans.
- Asymmetry: The Northern Hemisphere has a land-to-ocean ratio of 1:1.5, while the Southern Hemisphere is 1:4, providing a more moderate climate due to stored heat.

The Seven Continents:
- Asia: Largest continent, containing two-thirds of the world's population and features like the Himalayas and Dead Sea.
- Africa: Second largest, crossed by the equator, Tropic of Cancer, and Tropic of Capricorn, featuring the Sahara Desert and River Nile.
- North America: Third largest, linked to South America by the Isthmus of Panama.
- South America: Home to the Amazon River, Andes Mountains, and Angel Falls.
- Antarctica: Permanently ice-covered polar continent with no permanent human settlements.
- Europe: Second smallest continent, the birthplace of the Industrial Revolution.
- Australia: Smallest continent, driest inhabited landmass, and completely within the Southern Hemisphere.

The Five Oceans:
- Pacific Ocean: Largest ocean basin, containing the deepest point on Earth (Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench at ~10,924 m).
- Atlantic Ocean: Second largest, receiving the most freshwater runoff from major global rivers.
- Indian Ocean: Third largest, known for monsoon weather patterns and the warmest surface temperatures.
- Southern Ocean: Fourth largest and newest recognized ocean, extending from Antarctica to 60° S latitude.
- Arctic Ocean: Smallest ocean, largely covered by drifting polar icepacks.
