1. The Basic Economic Problems: Scarcity, Choice, and Opportunity Cost
1.1 Understanding Fundamental Economic Concepts

• Core Issue: Imbalance between limited resources and unlimited human wants, which leads to scarcity, necessitates choices, and incurs opportunity costs[cite: 6].
• Scarcity:
  ◦ Definition: The fundamental economic problem reflecting that all economic resources society wants to use are finite or limited in supply relative to unlimited human wants[cite: 6].
  ◦ Implication: Scarcity generates economic problems; if resources were fully abundant, no economic problems would exist[cite: 6].
  ◦ Scarcity vs. Shortage: Scarcity means available amount is less than wanted at zero price (universal and endless); a shortage occurs when people cannot get what they want at the prevailing price (specific and short-term)[cite: 6].
• Choice:
  ◦ Reason: Output is limited due to scarcity, so not all wants can be satisfied, forcing individuals, firms, and governments to make choices[cite: 6].
  ◦ Decisions: Must decide what output to produce, in what quantity, and what not to produce[cite: 6].
  ◦ Relationship: Scarcity implies choice, which in turn implies opportunity cost[cite: 6].
• Opportunity Cost:
  ◦ Definition: The amount or value of the next best alternative that must be sacrificed (forgone) to obtain one more unit of a product[cite: 6].
  ◦ Principle: Deciding to have more of one thing means deciding to have less of another[cite: 6].
  ◦ Key Points: Measured in terms of goods and services (not money); aligns with the principle of substitution[cite: 6].
  ◦ Summary: Arises from resource scarcity and alternative uses; when opportunity cost increases, people substitute other activities[cite: 6].

1.2 The Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF/PPC)

• Definition: A curve showing the various possible combinations of goods and services that a society can produce given its resources and technology[cite: 6].
• Assumptions: Fixed quantity and quality of resources, two broad classes of output, full employment/production, constant technology, and specialization exist[cite: 6].
• Related Terms:
  ◦ Production efficiency: Maximum level of output a manufacturer can produce without lowering another product's output[cite: 6].
  ◦ Resource allocation: Distribution of finite resources to specified purposes[cite: 6].
• PPF Graphical Layout:
  ◦ Points on the curve: Attainable and efficient[cite: 6].
  ◦ Points inside the curve: Attainable but inefficient[cite: 6].
  ◦ Points outside the curve: Unattainable with current resources and technology[cite: 6].
• Concepts Described: Illustrates scarcity (boundary limit), choice (movement along curve), and opportunity cost (downward slope)[cite: 6].
  ◦ Law of Increasing Opportunity Cost: As more of a product is produced, the opportunity cost per unit increases, making the PPF concave to the origin due to resource specialization[cite: 6].

1.3 Economic Growth and the PPF

• Definition: An increase in the total output level of an economy[cite: 6].
• Conditions: Occurs due to an increase in the quantity/quality of economic resources or advances in technology[cite: 6].
• Representation: Outward shift of the PPF[cite: 6].
• Asymmetric Growth: Growth can occur in only one sector, shifting the PPF along just the Y-axis or X-axis[cite: 6].

2. Central Problems of Economies

• Basic Questions: Every modern society must answer what to produce, how to produce, and for whom to produce[cite: 6].

2.1 What Goods and Services Are to Be Produced?

• Also called: The problem of resource allocation[cite: 6].
• Choices: Deciding between consumption vs. capital goods, civil vs. military goods, and necessity vs. luxury goods[cite: 6].

2.2 How to Produce Goods and Services?

• Also called: The problem of choice of technique[cite: 6].
• Techniques:
  ◦ Labour-intensive techniques: Use more labor relative to capital per unit of output[cite: 6].
  ◦ Capital-intensive techniques: Use more capital relative to labor per unit of output[cite: 6].
• Determinant: Depends on available factor supplies and relative prices[cite: 6].

2.3 For Whom Goods and Services Are to Be Produced?

• Also called: The problem of national product distribution[cite: 6].
• Focus: Relates to how the material product is distributed among society members (e.g., prioritizing necessary goods for the entire population first)[cite: 6].

3. Economic Systems

• Definition: The organizational and institutional framework a society establishes to answer the basic economic questions, primarily classified based on resource ownership[cite: 6].
• Main Types: Traditional, capitalist, command, and mixed economies[cite: 6].

3.1 Traditional Economy

• Definition: Relies on customs, history, and time-honored beliefs to guide production and distribution[cite: 6].
• Activities & Trade: Based on agriculture, fishing, hunting, or gathering; typically uses barter instead of money and produces mainly for need without surplus[cite: 6].

3.2 Capitalist Economy (Capitalism)

• Definition: Means of production are privately owned, production is driven by private entrepreneurs for profit, and government intervention is negligible (also called free market or laissez-faire economy)[cite: 6].
• Main Features: Private property right, consumer sovereignty, competition, limited government involvement, self-interest motivation, and income inequalities[cite: 6].

3.3 Command Economy (Socialism)

• Definition: Economic institutions are owned and controlled by the state and operate under a centralized plan[cite: 6].
• Main Features: Collective ownership, central economic planning, strong government control, and relative equality of incomes[cite: 6].

3.4 Mixed Economy

• Definition: Combines characteristics of capitalism and socialism with a blend of private and public ownership and government control[cite: 6].
• Main Features: Co-existence of public and private sectors, economic planning for rapid development, and regulations to prevent excessive wealth concentration[cite: 6].

Unit Summary

• Basic Economic Problems: Stem from resource scarcity (unlimited wants vs. limited means)[cite: 6].
• Production Possibility Curve (PPC): Depicts maximum output combinations; law of increasing opportunity cost explains its concave shape[cite: 6].
• Four Basic Economic Systems: Traditional (customs), Capitalist (private ownership/markets), Command (state ownership/planning), and Mixed (public-private blend)[cite: 6].
