Unit 8: Basic Entrepreneurship
• This unit introduces the fundamental concepts of entrepreneurship, exploring what it means to be an entrepreneur, the processes involved, and the essential qualities and skills required for success. It also delves into identifying opportunities, the importance of teamwork and diversity, and the financial aspects of starting and running a business.

8.1 Definition of Enterprise, Entrepreneur, and Entrepreneurship
Enterprise
• Definition: An enterprise is a legal entity that has the right to conduct business independently.
  ◦ This includes the ability to:
    ▪ Enter into contracts.
    ▪ Own property.
    ▪ Incur liabilities.
    ▪ Establish bank accounts.

Entrepreneur
• Definition: An entrepreneur is a person who conceives and develops a business idea.
  ◦ They take on the risk associated with establishing an enterprise.
  ◦ The goal is to produce a product or service that satisfies customer needs.
• Examples:
  ◦ Bethlehem Tilahun Alemu: Founded SoleRebels, a shoe company using recycled tires. Named fastest-growing brand in Africa by Forbes in 2014.
  ◦ Haile Gebrselassie: Retired long-distance runner, businessman, owner of Marathon Motors (assembles Hyundai vehicles, including electric cars), and involved in hospitality, entertainment, real estate, agriculture, and technology. Employs over 3000 people.

Entrepreneurship
• Definition: Entrepreneurship is the process of identifying opportunities in the marketplace, gathering the necessary resources to pursue these opportunities, and investing those resources to exploit them for long-term gains.
  ◦ It involves creating incremental wealth by combining resources in new ways to start and operate an enterprise.
  ◦ It is a practice and a process that drives creativity, innovation, and the development and growth of enterprises.
  ◦ Engaging in entrepreneurship shifts individuals from being "job seekers" to "job creators," which is vital in countries with high unemployment rates.
  ◦ It requires significant creativity as the driving force behind innovation.
• Critical Elements of the Entrepreneurship Process:
  ◦ The ability to spot a potentially profitable opportunity.
  ◦ The ability to commercialise the perceived opportunity (also known as innovation).
  ◦ The ability to pursue it in the long term.
  ◦ The ability to pursue it methodically.
  ◦ Acceptance of failure as a possibility.

History of Entrepreneurship
• Ancient Period: The term "entrepreneur" referred to a person managing large commercial projects using provided resources.
• 17th Century: An entrepreneur was someone with a contractual agreement with the government to supply specific products or services, with a fixed contract price that determined profit or loss based on their effort.
• 18th Century:
  ◦ Richard Cantillon's Theory: Defined an entrepreneur as a risk-taker. Examples include merchants buying at known prices and selling at unknown prices, and farmers and professionals operating with risk.
  ◦ Differentiation: The entrepreneurial role was distinguished from the capital-providing role.
• Late 19th and Early 20th Centuries: Entrepreneurs were viewed from an economic perspective, organizing and operating an enterprise for personal gain.
• Mid-Twentieth Century: The concept of the entrepreneur as an inventor was established.
• Evolution of Perception: The understanding of "entrepreneur" has evolved from managing commercial projects to applying innovation in business ideas.

Social Entrepreneurship
• Definition: Social entrepreneurship is an emerging field focused on developing solutions that address social issues. It attracts talent and resources with the aim of driving social change and providing benefits to society.
  ◦ Social entrepreneurs identify business opportunities that have a positive impact on their community, society, or the world.
  ◦ While gaining popularity, the exact definition can be broad, encompassing various socially beneficial activities.
  ◦ It indicates a commanding role in driving social change with a potential payoff for society.

8.2 Creativity and Innovation in Solving Local Problems
• Creativity and innovation are recognized as crucial drivers of economic growth and are intrinsically linked to the entrepreneurial process.

Creativity
• Definition: Creativity is the tendency to generate ideas, alternatives, or possibilities that can be useful for problem-solving, communication, or entertainment.
  ◦ It is the ability to conceive new ideas and identify novel ways of looking at problems and opportunities.
  ◦ It is a process of assembling ideas by recombining existing elements that might be assumed to be unrelated.
• Key Elements of Creativity:
  ◦ Ongoing Process: Creativity is continuous.
  ◦ Ideas: It results in ideas with potential value.
  ◦ Recombining: It involves putting things together in unexpected ways.
• To be Creative: One must be able to view things from new perspectives. Creativity is the development of original and potentially useful ideas for products, practices, services, or procedures.

Innovation
• Definition: Innovation is the implementation of a new idea at an individual, group, or organizational level.
  ◦ It lies at the heart of the entrepreneurial process and is essential for exploiting opportunities.
• Four Distinct Types of Innovation:
  ◦ Invention: The creation of a new product, service, or process.
  ◦ Expansion: The growth of an existing product, service, or process.
  ◦ Duplication: The replication of an already existing product, service, or process.
  ◦ Synthesis: The combination of existing concepts and factors into a new formulation.

From Creativity to Entrepreneurship
• Relationship:
  ◦ Creativity generates new ideas and perspectives on problems/opportunities.
  ◦ Innovation applies these creative solutions to improve lives or enrich society.
  ◦ Creativity leads to the generation of novel and innovative business approaches.
  ◦ Exploring new niches and ideas through creativity and innovation leads to efficiency, giving entrepreneurs a competitive advantage.

8.3 Entrepreneurial Attitudes, Behaviour, and Mind-set
• An entrepreneurial mind-set is a crucial state of mind that enables individuals to undertake entrepreneurial activities, characterized by specific attitudes and behaviors that increase the chances of business success.

Entrepreneurial Mind-set
• Definition: An entrepreneurial mind-set is a state of mind characterized by how individuals think and their attitude towards pursuing goals.
  ◦ It is an important tool for undertaking entrepreneurial activities.
  ◦ Entrepreneurs, due to their thinking and mind-set, give their businesses the best chance of survival.
  ◦ They are brave in their decision-making, actively seek clients, and take risks.
  ◦ A key aspect is learning from failures and exploiting them.

Key Attitudes for Entrepreneurs
• Passion: Entrepreneurs should be passionate about their ideas, goals, and companies. This passion is the driving force behind their business endeavors.
• Bravery: While acknowledging the fear of failure, entrepreneurs do not let it inhibit them. They learn from failures and use the fear to push themselves to work harder and correct mistakes. Bravery drives them to pursue successful businesses.
• Flexibility: Entrepreneurs encounter obstacles and difficulties. They need flexible mindsets to adapt when situations seem to lead towards failure. Flexible entrepreneurs modify their approach to achieve their established goals successfully.
• Strong Work Ethics: Starting a business requires many hours of hard work, frustration, creativity, and supervision. Entrepreneurs consistently work on developing new ideas, creating products, designing processes, and hiring talent.
• Integrity: Entrepreneurs must be trustworthy and honest. Colleagues, sellers, customers, and investors need to trust them for timely payments, reliable product/service delivery, a sense of value for employees, and potential for growth for investors.

Skills and Attributes for Innovation
• Skill: A skill is knowledge demonstrated through action; the ability to perform in a certain way. An entrepreneur turns a good business idea into reality using skills.
• Two Main Categories of Skills:
  ◦ General Management Skills: Abilities required to organize a company's physical and financial resources.
    ▪ Strategy Skills: Thinking about the business as a whole, its market position, value delivery, and competitive advantage.
    ▪ Planning Skills: Considering future impacts on the business and preparing accordingly.
    ▪ Marketing Skills: Understanding how products/services satisfy customer needs and why customers find them attractive.
    ▪ Financial Skills: Managing money, tracking expenses, monitoring cash flow, and evaluating investment risks.
    ▪ Project Management Skills: Organizing projects with specific objectives and timelines, ensuring resources are available at the right time.
    ▪ Time Management Skills: Using time productively, prioritizing tasks, and completing them on schedule.
  ◦ People Management Skills: Essential for guiding and committing people within and outside the organization (customers, suppliers, investors).
    ▪ Communication Skills: Expressing and informing others effectively, both orally and in writing.
    ▪ Leadership Skills: Inspiring people to work towards business success and complete required tasks.
    ▪ Motivational Skills: Enticing and compelling people to complete tasks by understanding their drivers and job expectations.
    ▪ Delegation Skills: Assigning tasks to others, understanding their skills, and how they can be used to meet business needs.
    ▪ Negotiation Skills: Understanding situational requirements, others' motivations, and identifying opportunities for mutually beneficial outcomes.
• Intertwined Nature: These people skills are interconnected. Entrepreneurial performance results from a combination of business knowledge, general management skills, people skills, and personal motivation.
• Continuous Improvement: Entrepreneurs should continuously work to improve their skills, identify strengths and weaknesses, and plan for future development.

8.4 Windows of Entrepreneurial Opportunities
• Identifying and capitalizing on entrepreneurial opportunities is a critical step for aspiring entrepreneurs, requiring a specific mindset and a systematic approach to discerning viable business ideas.

Entrepreneurial Opportunity
• Definition: An entrepreneurial opportunity exists when identifiable consumer demand meets the possibility of satisfying that demand with a product or service.
  ◦ An idea must be convertible into an opportunity to be worth pursuing.
  ◦ This involves developing the right mindset: sharpening senses for consumer needs and conducting research to validate the idea's potential.
• Discovery Methods:
  ◦ Deliberate Search: Especially when developing new technologies.
  ◦ Chance/Unintentional Emergence: Opportunities arising unexpectedly.
  ◦ Problem Recognition: Recognizing a problem and making a concerted effort to solve it.
• Joseph Schumpeter's Theory ("Creative Destruction"):
  ◦ Entrepreneurial innovation is a disruptive force that creates and sustains economic growth.
  ◦ It can also destroy established companies, reshape businesses, and disrupt employment.
  ◦ Business process changes, including "creative destruction," aim to increase company efficiency and improve lifestyles, though they can make older activities or products obsolete.

Methods for Finding New Business Opportunities (Schumpeter)
• Develop a new market for an existing product.
• Find a new source of resources that allows for low-cost product manufacturing.
• Use existing technology to produce an old product in a new way.
• Use existing technology to produce a new product.
• Use new technology to produce a new product.
• Opportunity Types:
  ◦ Demand Opportunities: Related to supply or demand.
  ◦ Technological Innovations: The last three methods listed above fall into this category.

8.5 Entrepreneurial Success, Teamwork, and Diversity
• While individual ability is important, teamwork is increasingly recognized as a critical factor in business success, bringing numerous benefits and fostering a positive company culture. Diversity within teams further enhances creativity and performance.

Importance of Teamwork to Business Success
• Modern Emphasis: Many companies now highlight the importance of teamwork, recognizing that even successful leaders need strong teams to achieve overall success.
• Team Building Investment: Companies are investing in team-building strategies due to the growing emphasis on teamwork's value.

Benefits of Teamwork
• Fosters Cooperation:
  ◦ Cooperation among teammates is a key element of success.
  ◦ Sharing experiences and ideas leads to better results for individuals and the company.
  ◦ Teams that prioritize shared goals and collaboration achieve better outcomes than individuals working in isolation.
• Broadens Perspectives:
  ◦ Working alone offers a single perspective, which can be limiting.
  ◦ Teams provide diverse perspectives and ideas, leading to potentially better results.
  ◦ Diversity: Teams with members from diverse backgrounds (gender, age, ethnicity, etc.) are more creative and perform better (up to 35% higher) than non-diverse teams. Teamwork offers a more comprehensive, multi-dimensional view of issues.
• Increases Productivity:
  ◦ Workload is shared among team members with different knowledge and skills.
  ◦ Coordinated efforts allow tasks to be completed more efficiently and in a shorter time than by a single person.
  ◦ Different problem-solving methods generate new ideas and solutions that might be difficult for an individual to develop.
  ◦ Effective brainstorming sessions foster innovative ideas and allow team members to build on each other's contributions.
• Provides Opportunities for Learning:
  ◦ Team members with different skills, experience levels, and abilities exchange knowledge while discussing work.
  ◦ They grow together and improve their abilities through collaborative work.
• Shapes Company Culture:
  ◦ Well-functioning teams foster friendship, loyalty, and cooperation, shaping the company's culture.
  ◦ A positive work environment boosts morale, happiness, and satisfaction, creating a sense of belonging.
  ◦ The team becomes a community where members understand and play their roles towards a common goal.
  ◦ Mutual support builds relationships based on trust and respect, making employees more reliable, effective, and productive, ultimately contributing to company success.

8.6 Finance and Promotion of Entrepreneurship
• Securing adequate financial resources and managing them wisely are essential for entrepreneurial ventures, with various budgetary requirements and financing options available to support business development.

Financial Requirements for Entrepreneurial Ventures
• Budgetary Needs: All businesses require money for various activities. The type and adequacy of available funds necessitate appropriate funding methods.
  ◦ Permanent Capital:
    ▪ Typically comes from investment in share companies or personal loans to partners.
    ▪ Used to finance start-up costs, major developments, and expansions.
    ▪ May be needed for significant innovations like new product development.
    ▪ Ideally repaid when the firm can afford it; equity investments are rewarded by dividends or capital gains.
  ◦ Working Capital:
    ▪ A type of short-term financing.
    ▪ Needed by most small firms to pay for raw materials and other operating costs.
    ▪ Requirements vary significantly by business type.
    ▪ Can sometimes be sufficient to finance the start-up of a small business, with suppliers effectively financing it.
    ▪ While typically used for trading, it can also fund short-lived assets (e.g., vehicles replaced every 4-5 years).
  ◦ Asset Finance:
    ▪ A medium- to long-term loan.
    ▪ Used to finance the purchase of physical assets (plants, machinery, equipment, vehicles, buildings).
    ▪ Financing is typically spread over a longer period, from 3 to 10 years or more, depending on the asset's useful life.

Financing Options
• Importance: Financial resources are vital, but needs evolve as an enterprise grows. Obtaining sufficient capital at the right time can be challenging for entrepreneurial projects due to their perceived risk. Financing involves wise asset management for capital efficiency.
• Key Issue: Ensuring sufficient cash flow for operations and planning financing to match enterprise changes.
• Sources of Finance:
  ◦ Personal Savings: Entrepreneurs should ideally contribute at least half of the start-up funds from their own savings.
  ◦ Friends and Relatives: After personal savings, entrepreneurs can approach friends and relatives who may be willing to invest, provided the opportunities and risks are clearly explained.
  ◦ Partners: Entrepreneurs can take on partners to increase the capital formation for the business.
  ◦ Angels (Angel Investors): Wealthy individuals, often entrepreneurs themselves, who invest in start-up businesses in exchange for risk-sharing.
  ◦ Venture Capital Firms: Private organizations that invest in new businesses with high growth potential and the expectation of high returns. They provide start-up, development, or expansion funds.

Unit Summary
• An entrepreneur is an individual who creates a new business, assuming most risks and enjoying most rewards.
• Entrepreneurship is the process of setting up a business. Entrepreneurs are often innovators, sources of new ideas, goods, services, and business procedures.
• Social entrepreneurship focuses on driving social change and addressing social issues, offering lasting benefits to society. Social entrepreneurs explore business opportunities with a positive community or global impact.
• Entrepreneurs can detect and capitalize on business opportunities, developing new products and utilizing innovative manufacturing methods in various markets.
• Creativity is the ability to generate new ideas and discover different perspectives on problems and opportunities. Creative entrepreneurs are investors in talent.
• Innovation is central to entrepreneurship, representing the process of renewing and updating products or services by applying new processes, techniques, or ideas to create new value.
