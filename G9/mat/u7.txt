7.1 Revision on Congruency of Triangles
Introduction to Congruency

Congruency describes the concept of equality in size and shape between geometric figures. If two figures are congruent, one can be transformed into the other using turns, flips, and/or slides, meaning they are identical in every aspect.

    Key Idea: Congruent figures have exactly the same shape and size.

Definition of Congruency

    Definition 7.1: When two triangles have exactly the same three sides and the same three angles, they are called congruent triangles.

    Corresponding Parts:
        "Same three sides" means the corresponding sides have equal length.
        "Same three angles" means the corresponding angles have equal measure.

Conditions for Congruency of Triangles (Congruency Postulates/Theorems)

To determine if two triangles are congruent, we don't always need to check all three sides and all three angles. Specific conditions are sufficient.

    SSS Congruency (Side-Side-Side)
        Condition: When two triangles have equal corresponding sides, then the triangles are congruent.
        Explanation: If all three sides of one triangle are congruent to the corresponding three sides of another triangle, the triangles are congruent.
    SAS Congruency (Side-Angle-Side)
        Condition: When two triangles have two pairs of congruent sides and one pair of congruent angles between the sides (the included angle), then the triangles are congruent.
        Explanation: The angle must be included between the two sides whose congruency is established.
    ASA Congruency (Angle-Side-Angle)
        Condition: When two triangles have two pairs of congruent angles and one pair of congruent sides between the angles (the included side), then the triangles are congruent.
        Explanation: The side must be included between the two angles whose congruency is established.
    RHS Congruency (Right-angle Hypotenuse Side)
        Condition: When two right triangles have an equal hypotenuse and one equal corresponding side (leg), then the triangles are congruent.
        Explanation: This condition applies specifically to right-angled triangles.

7.2 Definition of Similar Figures
Introduction to Similarity

Similar figures share the same shape but not necessarily the same size. One figure can be seen as a scaled version (magnified or diminished) of the other.
Definition of Similarity

    Definition 7.2: Two plane figures are similar if their corresponding angles are congruent and the ratios of their corresponding sides are proportional. This common ratio is called the scale factor.

    Key Properties of Similar Figures:
        All corresponding angles are congruent (equal in measure).
        All corresponding sides are proportional (their ratios are equal).
    Magnification/Diminution: When similar figures are magnified or diminished, one can be exactly placed on the other.
    Notation: The symbol '

    Examples of Always Similar Figures:
        Any two circles (regardless of their radii) are always similar because they have the same shape.
        Any two squares (regardless of their side lengths) are always similar.
        Any two regular polygons (with the same number of sides) are similar. For instance, all equilateral triangles are similar, and all regular pentagons are similar.
