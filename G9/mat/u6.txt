        Sides: The line segments forming the polygon.
        Vertex: The common end point of any two sides.
        Interior Angles: The angles formed inside the polygon.

    In general, a polygon with 𝑛 sides is called an "𝑛-gon".

Definition of Convex and Concave Polygons

    Convex Polygon: A polygon where the measure of each interior angle is less than 180 degrees. The vertices of a convex polygon always point outwards.

    Concave Polygon: A polygon where at least one interior angle has a measure more than 180 degrees. The vertices of a concave polygon point inwards and outwards.

    Alternative Description: If none of the side extensions intersects the polygon, then the polygon is convex; otherwise it is concave.

    Key Takeaway: Convex polygons have all interior angles less than 180°, while concave polygons have at least one interior angle greater than 180°.

6.4 Properties of Regular Polygons: Pentagon, Hexagon, Octagon, and Decagon
Line of Symmetry for Regular Polygons

    Symmetry: When two parts of a figure are identical after folding or flipping. To be symmetrical, one half must be the mirror image of the other.

    Line of Symmetry: A line through which the two halves of the figure match exactly. Also called a mirror line. A figure can have more than one line of symmetry. An asymmetrical figure has no line of symmetry.

    Symmetry in Regular Polygons: All regular polygons are symmetrical.
        An equilateral triangle has 3 lines of symmetry.
        A square has 4 lines of symmetry.
        A regular pentagon has 5 lines of symmetry.
        A regular hexagon has 6 lines of symmetry.
        The lines of symmetry meet at a point inside the polygon called the center of the polygon.

    Lines of Symmetry Based on Number of Sides:
        If 𝑛 (number of sides) is odd, the line of symmetry connects a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.
        If 𝑛 is even, the line of symmetry connects a vertex to the opposite vertex, and the midpoint of a side to the midpoint of the opposite side.

    Key Property: An 𝑛-sided regular polygon has 𝑛 lines of symmetry.

Inscribed and Circumscribed Polygons

    Circumcircle: A circle that passes through all the vertices of a polygon.

    Incircle: A circle inside a polygon that touches each side of the polygon at its midpoint. The radius of the incircle is the apothem of the polygon.

    Apothem: A line segment from the center of a regular polygon perpendicular to a side (and bisecting that side).

    A circle can be inscribed in and circumscribed about any regular polygon.
