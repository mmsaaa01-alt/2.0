Summary of Unit 7 Key Concepts

This summary encapsulates the fundamental principles of congruency and similarity.

    Similar Plane Figures: Two plane figures are similar if:
        i. Their corresponding sides are proportional.
        ii. Their corresponding angles are congruent.
    Enlargement Properties: Under an enlargement transformation:
        a. Lines and their images are parallel.
        b. Angles remain the same (are preserved).
        c. All lengths are increased or decreased in the same ratio.
    Scale Factor: The ratio of corresponding sides, usually expressed numerically, calculated as:

    Triangle Similarity Theorems:
        AA (Angle-Angle) Similarity Theorem: If two angles of one triangle are congruent to the corresponding two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are similar.
        SSS (Side-Side-Side) Similarity Theorem: If the corresponding three sides of two triangles are proportional to each other, then the triangles are similar.
        SAS (Side-Angle-Side) Similarity Theorem: If the corresponding two sides of two triangles are proportional and the included angles are congruent, then the two triangles are similar.
    Perimeter Ratio of Similar Figures: If two figures are similar, the ratio of their perimeters is equal to the ratio of their corresponding side lengths.
    Area Ratio of Similar Figures: If two figures are similar, the ratio of their areas is equal to the square of the ratio of their corresponding side lengths.

Definition 8.1: Scalars

    Example 1: Mass

Definition 8.2: Vectors

    Vectors: Quantities that are fully described by both a magnitude and a direction.

    Many engineering applications rely on vectors. Force, acceleration, velocity, electric fields, and magnetic fields are all vector quantities.

    The position of a projectile or a robot in computer software is also described by a vector, requiring both its distance and direction from a reference point.

    Example 4: Force
        Force causes an object to move or speed up. When a force is applied, the effect depends not only on its strength (magnitude) but also on the direction in which it acts.
        For example, a force of 10 Newtons applied vertically upward has a different effect than the same force applied horizontally. The direction is crucial, indicating that force is a vector quantity.

    Example 5: Displacement vs. Distance
        Distance is a scalar quantity, only referring to "how much ground an object has covered."
        Displacement is a vector quantity, representing "directed distance" - the shortest distance from the initial to the final position, along with a specified direction.
