Citizenship Education: Applied Ethics & The Culture of Using Digital Technology

This chapter explores the concepts of applied ethics, focusing on professional and environmental ethics, and then transitions to understanding digital technology, its impact on culture, its various purposes, and the need for ethical conduct in its use.[cite: 5]

1. Applied Ethics: Understanding Moral Dilemmas in Society[cite: 5]

This section introduces applied ethics as a field for addressing moral challenges in various social contexts, with a specific focus on professional responsibilities and environmental stewardship.[cite: 5]

1.1 The Meaning of Applied Ethics[cite: 5]

• Applied Ethics: The art or science of reflecting on moral dilemmas and moral problems in different social contexts[cite: 5].
• Nature: It is a growing, interdisciplinary field that addresses ethical problems in various societal areas[cite: 5].
• Examples of Fields:[cite: 5]
  ◦ Social and political ethics[cite: 5]
  ◦ Computer ethics[cite: 5]
  ◦ Medical ethics[cite: 5]
  ◦ Bioethics[cite: 5]
  ◦ Environmental ethics[cite: 5]
  ◦ Business ethics[cite: 5]
  ◦ Professional ethics[cite: 5]
• Perspectives:[cite: 5]
  ◦ From Ethics: It is a specialization within the broader field of ethics[cite: 5].
  ◦ From Social Practice: It involves focusing on the ethical aspects and implications of a particular social practice[cite: 5].

1.2 Professional Ethics[cite: 5]

• Profession: Any occupation, job, or vocation that requires advanced expertise (skills and knowledge), self-regulation, and concerned service to the public good[cite: 5].
  ◦ It typically brings high social and economic status[cite: 5].
• Characteristics of Professionals:[cite: 5]
  ◦ Governed by codes of ethics[cite: 5].
  ◦ Commitment to:[cite: 5]
    - Competence[cite: 5]
    - Integrity[cite: 5]
    - Altruism (selfless concern for the well-being of others)[cite: 5]
    - Promotion of the public good within their domain[cite: 5].
• Social Contract: These commitments form the basis of a social contract between a profession and society, meaning professionals are accountable to those they serve and to society as a whole[cite: 5].
• Professional Ethics: Concerned with one's behavior, responsibility, and commitment needed during the provision of professional services[cite: 5].
  ◦ Refers to the ethical norms, values, and principles that guide a profession[cite: 5].
  ◦ It's about the moral obligations, rules, and principles professionals should use to achieve their profession's main objective[cite: 5].
• Example: Teaching Profession[cite: 5]
  ◦ One professional ethic implies a teacher's smooth relationships with:[cite: 5]
    - Students[cite: 5]
    - Colleagues[cite: 5]
    - Employers[cite: 5]
    - Students' parents (relevant stakeholders)[cite: 5]
  ◦ These principles guide teachers in their daily activities[cite: 5].
• Important Attributes of Professional Ethics:[cite: 5]
  ◦ Honesty[cite: 5]
  ◦ Integrity[cite: 5]
  ◦ Transparency[cite: 5]
  ◦ Confidentiality[cite: 5]
  ◦ Impartiality[cite: 5]
  ◦ Punctuality[cite: 5]
  ◦ Respectfulness[cite: 5]
  ◦ Obedience to the law[cite: 5]

1.3 Environmental Ethics[cite: 5]

• Environment: The surrounding natural world which contains both living and nonliving things such as land, animals, plants, water, air, etc[cite: 5].
  ◦ It is the foundation and source of survival for all living things[cite: 5].
  ◦ Can also be understood as the overall economic, social, and cultural conditions influencing an individual's or community's life[cite: 5].
• Environmental Ethics: Refers to the moral obligations expected from human beings to protect and maintain the well-being of their environment[cite: 5].
  ◦ It is the discipline that studies the established values, principles, and beliefs concerning the relationship between human beings and the surrounding environment[cite: 5].
• Direct Relationship between Humans and Environment:[cite: 5]
  ◦ When the environment is safe and healthy, humans lead good and healthy lives[cite: 5].
  ◦ When the environment is damaged, human life is directly affected[cite: 5].
• Current Threat: The world faces a serious threat of climate change and environmental degradation, which is worsening over time[cite: 5].
• Importance of Environmental Well-being: Giving great focus to the well-being of the environment is not optional; it is a matter of survival[cite: 5].
  ◦ Human life fully depends on the environment and finite Earth resources[cite: 5].
• Our Duty: All individuals have a duty to play their part in maintaining and protecting the well-being of the environment for the sake of survival[cite: 5].

2. The Culture of Using Digital Technology[cite: 5]

This unit explores the concept of digital technology, its relationship with culture, its various purposes in daily life, and the ethical codes of conduct required for its responsible use[cite: 5].

2.1 The Concept of Digital Technology[cite: 5]

• Digital Technology: Refers to the use of computers, tablets, mobile phones, and digital and social media that are accessed on those devices through the internet[cite: 5].
  ◦ May also involve new communication technologies with significant effects on political, economic, social, and cultural life[cite: 5].
• Role: Plays significant roles in transferring information and data quickly around the world and among individuals[cite: 5].
  ◦ This occurs through mobile applications, social media platforms, and digital marketing websites[cite: 5].
• Pervasiveness: We live in an age where life heavily relies on different types of digital technologies, touching every aspect of life and making it easier and better[cite: 5].
  ◦ High interconnection between individuals and global societies through social networks and media platforms[cite: 5].
  ◦ Many people use electronic devices (computers, mobile phones) and the internet daily[cite: 5].
• History: Digital technology began in the mid-20th century[cite: 5].
• Modern Significance: It has become the most significant modern technology in both developed and developing countries, including Ethiopia[cite: 5].
• Importance in Daily Life: Crucially important for:[cite: 5]
  ◦ Communication speed[cite: 5]
  ◦ Learning opportunities[cite: 5]
  ◦ Social relations[cite: 5]
  ◦ Money exchange in the finance sector[cite: 5]
• Common Digital Technologies Today:[cite: 5]
  ◦ Computer[cite: 5]
  ◦ Smartwatch[cite: 5]
  ◦ Automated Teller Machine (ATM)[cite: 5]
  ◦ Digital Camera[cite: 5]
  ◦ Automated vehicles[cite: 5]
  ◦ Drone[cite: 5]
  ◦ Bitcoin[cite: 5]
  ◦ GPS[cite: 5]
• Dependence in Contemporary Ethiopia: Easy and fast communication, money transfer, banking, and access to education are often impossible without digital technology[cite: 5].
  ◦ As digital citizens, individual life is highly dependent on modern digital media technologies for tasks like:[cite: 5]
    - Mobile phone communication with family[cite: 5]
    - Online services via the internet[cite: 5]
    - Mobile banking money transfer at any time[cite: 5]
• Benefits: These technologies make life easier and better by saving time and reducing physical contact for various services[cite: 5].

2.2 Culture and Digital Technology[cite: 5]

• Interdependence: Culture and technology are major factors shaping our world outlooks and greatly influencing our attitudes, beliefs, behaviors, and understanding of good and bad[cite: 5].
  ◦ Society's cultures are the bases of human civilization and technological advancement[cite: 5].
  ◦ Cultural values and modern technologies are very interdependent[cite: 5].
  ◦ When new technologies are introduced, culture reacts in either a positive or negative way[cite: 5].
• Positive Impacts: Promoting and Preserving Culture[cite: 5]
  ◦ Cultural development and digital technologies are directly interlinked in making cultures accessible to others[cite: 5].
  ◦ Communication: Essential for living in societies and maintaining culture, as cultural elements are shared through communication[cite: 5].
  ◦ Sharing Ideas and Feelings: Communication technologies are used to share ideas and feelings, enabling culture to be learned, transmitted, and preserved[cite: 5].
  ◦ Global Reach: Promotes one's own cultural values and allows understanding of other societies' cultures[cite: 5].
  ◦ Difficult to promote and preserve culture effectively without communication media in this digital world[cite: 5].
  ◦ Highlighting Ethiopia's Culture: Digital technologies are crucial in highlighting the bright aspects of a multicultural country like Ethiopia to the world[cite: 5].
    - Nations and nationalities can promote and preserve their cultures using digital communication technologies[cite: 5].
    - Examples of Digital Platforms: YouTube, Facebook, Twitter, Telegram, Imo, etc[cite: 5].
    - Untold Facts to Promote: Language, way of life, cultural dressing, cultural food, arts, historical tourism sites, holiday celebrations, etc[cite: 5].
• Negative Impacts: Cultural Diffusion and Destruction[cite: 5]
  ◦ Digital technologies can have serious negative impacts if not used appropriately and responsibly[cite: 5].
  ◦ Deviation from Norms: Using technology in ways that deviate from moral values and principles can lead to cultural diffusion and the destruction of indigenous values and norms[cite: 5].
  ◦ Example: Inappropriate use can affect family relationships, e.g., individuals being engrossed in social media, ignoring family members[cite: 5].
• Digital Culture: Must be in line with the existing moral, social, and cultural values of the society in the contemporary digital age[cite: 5].

2.3 The Purpose of Digital Technology[cite: 5]

Digital technologies are integral to daily life, offering efficiency and effectiveness. Here are their major purposes in the 21st century[cite: 5].

2.3.1 Social Connection[cite: 5]

• Importance: Information is indispensable; connecting families, relatives, and community members is crucial for healthy social life[cite: 5].
• Simplification: Digital technology has simplified socialization by enabling communication with family, friends, and co-workers remotely[cite: 5].
• Communication Methods: Allows communication via words, video (video calls, virtual conferences), and media exchange[cite: 5].
• Modern Necessity: Leading life is becoming very difficult without digital devices and information communication technology[cite: 5].

2.3.2 Communication Speed[cite: 5]

• Information Compression: Digital technology allows large amounts of information to be compressed onto small, transportable storage devices[cite: 5].
• Internet Speed: Constantly improving internet speed enables instant transfer of tons of information and virtual data access anywhere[cite: 5].
• Transformation: Improves transmission speed and has transformed how people communicate, work, and learn[cite: 5].
• Global Impact: Enables speedy communication and information exchange among global communities in fractions of microseconds, making life better[cite: 5].
• Example: Email communication is vital for individuals and organizations[cite: 5].

2.3.3 Online Learning Opportunities[cite: 5]

• Integration: In modern society, educators must integrate digital media due to its daily and seamless use by people[cite: 5].
• Instant Access: Internet access provides immediate access to diverse information within seconds through online searches[cite: 5].
• Educational Resources: Digital technology enables everyone to access:[cite: 5]
  ◦ Online courses[cite: 5]
  ◦ Training[cite: 5]
  ◦ Books[cite: 5]
  ◦ Journals[cite: 5]
  ◦ Publications[cite: 5]
  ◦ Other important information[cite: 5]
• Engaging Learning: Online education facilities and interactive media have made learning processes very interesting[cite: 5].
• Accessibility: Using digital technology allows access to education online without needing to physically go to a learning center[cite: 5].

2.3.4 Digital Marketing[cite: 5]

• Definition: The promotion of your business using online resources and tools[cite: 5].
• Tools: Includes websites, micro-sites, mobile applications, and social media platforms[cite: 5].
• Benefits for Businesses:[cite: 5]
  ◦ 24/7 Sales: Online selling allows customers to buy whenever they want, even when physical shops are closed[cite: 5].
  ◦ Efficiency: Typically helps reduce internal processing times, freeing staff for other business areas[cite: 5].
  ◦ Customer Access: Provides customers access to businesses through their preferred means (mobile, desktop, phone call, social media) to find information, services, or products[cite: 5].

Unit Summary[cite: 5]

• This unit introduced ethics as a discipline concerning what humans ought and ought not to do, focusing on standards, values, and principles that differentiate right from wrong[cite: 5].

Glossary[cite: 5]

• Abiding by the law: The willingness, commitment, and responsibility to obey or follow the rules and regulations[cite: 5].
• Commitment to excellence: Taking full responsibility for our behaviors, decisions, and actions or endeavors in every single situation in order to do great things or being excellent and resilient person in our life[cite: 5].
• Concern for others: The habits of having a place for, listening to, or considering the needs and feelings of others[cite: 5].
• Digital Technology: Type of technology used to transfer information to move data quickly. They include all electronic tools, automatic systems, technological devices and resources that generate and process or store information[cite: 5].
• Environmental Ethics: The moral obligations expected from human beings to protect and maintain the well-being of their environment[cite: 5].
• Ethics: Moral principles that control or influence a person's behavior. The rules or standards governing the conduct of a person or the members of a profession[cite: 5].
• Integrity: Uncompromising devotion to moral and ethical principles regarding our decisions and actions in our daily life. The quality of being honest or having strong moral principles[cite: 5].
• Keeping promise: Fulfilling or being faithful to a promise one has made or keeping a verbal commitment to do something to someone else[cite: 5].
• Loyalty: The attribute of being truthful/honest to someone else[cite: 5].
• Morality: The standards and principles used to determine the behaviors, decisions or actions of individuals as right or wrong, good or bad, acceptable or unacceptable[cite: 5].
• Profession: Any occupation/job/vocation that requires advanced expertise (skills and knowledge), self-regulation, and concerned service to the public good[cite: 5].
• Professional Ethics: The moral obligations, rules, and principles that professionals should employ to achieve the main objective of their profession[cite: 5].
• Respecting others: Having positive feelings toward a person or treating a person with dignity[cite: 5].
• Values: Specific principles, beliefs, and standards, ideals that one holds as important and right[cite: 5].
