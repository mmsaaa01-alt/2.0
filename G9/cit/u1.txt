Citizenship Education: Ethics and Ethical Values

This chapter introduces the fundamental concepts of ethics and morality, explores major ethical values crucial for citizens, and highlights the significant importance of these values in fostering a peaceful, stable, and prosperous society.

1. The Meaning of Ethics

This section defines ethics, differentiates it from morality, and explains its role in guiding human behavior and decisions.

1.1 Defining Ethics

• Historical Origin: The term ethics originates from the Greek word ethos, which refers to the customs, habits, and mores of people.
• Core Definition:
  ◦ Ethics is a discipline that deals with morality.
  ◦ It is a set of moral principles that guide conduct.
• Theoretical/Descriptive View: From this perspective, ethics is concerned with understanding the nature and origin of moral judgments. It asks "What is morality?"
• Practical/Prescriptive View: From this perspective, ethics focuses on the norms, values, or principles that ought to be used to make proper moral judgments. It asks "What should we do?"
• Everyday Application: Ethical questions often involve deciding what one should do in a specific situation. Judgments about these decisions are expressed using words like:
  ◦ Right and Wrong
  ◦ Should and Ought
  ◦ Obligation and Duty

1.2 Ethics vs. Morality

• Morality: Refers to the standards and principles used to determine if individual behaviors, decisions, or actions are:
  ◦ Right or wrong
  ◦ Good or bad
  ◦ Acceptable or unacceptable
• Ethics: Is concerned with what is right or wrong, good or bad, or acceptable or not acceptable in a broader sense, often providing the framework for understanding morality.

1.3 Ethics in Daily Life

• Human Conduct: Ethics is fundamentally about human conduct and is applied in daily life to determine acceptable human behavior.
• Societal Good: It is concerned with what is beneficial for both individuals and society as a whole.
• Impact on Behavior: Ethics influences behavior and requires individuals to choose the right options.
• Importance for Responsibility: Managing life and acting responsibly is extremely challenging without ethical guidance.
• Refraining from Unethical Acts: Ethics obliges individuals to avoid harmful or unethical acts, such as:
  ◦ Rape
  ◦ Stealing
  ◦ Murder
  ◦ Assault
  ◦ Slander
  ◦ Fraud
• Virtues and Rights: Ethics also encompasses important virtues and standards related to rights:
  ◦ Virtues: Honesty, compassion, loyalty.
  ◦ Rights: The right to life, the right to freedom from injury, the right to privacy.
• Guiding Principle: Generally, ethics is a discipline that teaches us to:
  ◦ Follow the right way.
  ◦ Do the right thing.
  ◦ Refrain from bad or wrongdoings.

• Key Takeaway: Ethics helps us distinguish between moral and immoral actions, guides responsible living, and teaches us to act in ways that benefit ourselves and society while upholding fundamental rights and virtues.

2. Major Ethical Values

This section delves into specific ethical values that impose obligations on individuals to act rightly and avoid wrongdoing. Values serve as a moral compass, guiding decisions about right and wrong.

2.1 Understanding Values

• Definition: Values are specific principles, beliefs, standards, and ideals that one holds as important and right.
• Decision-Making Tool: They are the rules by which individuals make decisions about what is right and wrong.
• Moral Compass: Values provide a moral direction or compass for an individual's life.

2.2 Integrity
2.2.1 What is Integrity?

• Definition: Integrity refers to devotion to moral and ethical principles regarding our decisions and actions in our daily life. It implies the quality of being honest or having strong moral principles.
• Consistency: A person has integrity when:
  ◦ Their behavior matches their beliefs.
  ◦ What they say and what they do match.
  ◦ Their private and public life aligns.
• Core Qualities: People with integrity are generally known to be:
  ◦ Trustworthy
  ◦ Honest
  ◦ Kind
• Citizen Responsibility: Developing and preserving moral integrity is a crucial responsibility for all citizens.
• Broader Responsibilities: A person with integrity has responsibilities towards:
  ◦ Humanity
  ◦ Themselves
  ◦ Their family
  ◦ Their society
  ◦ Their religion
  ◦ Their country
• Critical Thinking: Integrity involves thinking critically and basing behaviors and actions on moral values and principles, rather than solely on personal interest.

2.2.2 Attributes of a Person with True Integrity

Individuals with true integrity possess important qualities that everyone should strive for:

• A. Respect Humanity: They believe everyone deserves respect simply for being a human being, regardless of race, ethnicity, language, religion, place of birth, etc.
• B. Walk Their Talk: Their actions consistently match their words. They have the moral courage to do what they say and are committed to fulfilling their moral obligations in the community.
• C. Are Honest: They are devoted to facts and free from unethical behaviors such as lying, stealing, cheating, or corruption. An example is honestly paying taxes. They are honest with themselves and do not engage in activities that cheat or harm the public for self-interest.
• D. Take Responsibility for Their Actions: They accept accountability for their daily actions, seeing it as a moral excellence and a step towards success.
• E. Are Kind: They are benevolent, kind-hearted, and happy for the success of others. They are free from abnormal jealousy and do not exploit others.
• F. Know Well Their Strengths and Weaknesses: They appreciate their strengths and learn from their shortcomings for continuous self-improvement.
• G. Are Hard Workers: They believe that overcoming poverty and hardship is achieved through hard work.
• H. Free from Addiction: They avoid substance (alcohol, smoking, khat, drugs) and non-substance (gambling) addictions that negatively affect health, socio-economic status, and work performance.

2.2.3 How to Enhance Your Integrity

You can strengthen your integrity by:

• Making and keeping commitments to yourself.
• Standing for something - knowing your values and sticking to them.
• Being open - opening your mind, heart, and ears to new ideas and perspectives.

2.3 Promise Keeping

• Definition: Promise keeping refers to honoring a verbal commitment to do something for someone else.
• Sacrifice and Fulfillment: Keeping a promise often requires limiting or sacrificing one's own desires to fulfill an obligation to others.
• Building Relationships: Promises are actions that build:
  ◦ Love
  ◦ Strong friendships
  ◦ Smooth relationships with others
• Self-Improvement: Individuals can also make promises to themselves to improve their own lives (e.g., promising to stop smoking).
• Caution: While important, it is crucial not to make promises that are beyond one's ability to keep.

2.4 Loyalty

• Definition: Loyalty is the attribute of being truthful and honest to an individual, a society, an institution, or a country.
• Core Value: It is primarily a matter of conscience and is essential in various aspects of life.
• Importance: Loyalty is needed in:
  ◦ The workplace
  ◦ Between friends
  ◦ Between spouses
  ◦ Between a lender and a debtor
• Socio-Economic and Political Significance: Loyalty is a very essential ethical value for a country's socio-economic and political activities, holding great social and national value.
• Public Leaders: Individuals leading people and the country should be:
  ◦ Honest
  ◦ Trustworthy
  ◦ Loyal to their people
  ◦ Use public property fairly and justly
  ◦ Serve everyone equally, regardless of ethnicity, place of birth, religion, language, sex, etc.
• Employees/Professionals: Professionals (teachers, medical doctors, accountants, soldiers, engineers) should be loyal to their employers and the societies they serve.
• Good Citizens: As good citizens, we must be faithful in our:
  ◦ Family
  ◦ Neighborhood
  ◦ Community
  ◦ Friendship
  ◦ School
  ◦ Workplace
  ◦ Country at large

2.5 Concern for Others

• Definition: Concern for others refers to the habits of having a place for, listening to, or considering the needs and feelings of others.
• Participation and Support: It means:
  ◦ Enabling or allowing others to participate in decision-making processes that affect their political, economic, and social life.
  ◦ Supporting others during times of difficulty or hardship.
• Moral Responsibility: As citizens, it is our moral responsibility to be concerned for vulnerable sections of society who need support and care, such as:
  ◦ People with disabilities
  ◦ Children
  ◦ Elders
  ◦ Women
  ◦ People with HIV/AIDS
• Cultural Practices: Many societies have good cultures of mutual help in various social situations, for example:
  ◦ During wedding ceremonies
  ◦ Traditional cooperative systems (Debo/Jigi)
  ◦ When someone gives birth
  ◦ During mourning periods
  ◦ During natural and man-made disasters
  ◦ When a person is seriously sick
  ◦ When someone faces difficult economic problems

2.6 Respect for Others

• Common Moral Value: Respect is a well-known moral value practiced in families, communities, and schools (e.g., showing respect to parents, elders, teachers, friends, neighbors).
• Definition: Respect is having positive feelings toward a person or treating a person with dignity, regardless of ethnicity, religion, language, color, sex, economic background, disability, and age (being a child or an adult).
• Universal Respect: We must respect each other simply because we are human beings.
• Ways to Show Respect: We can be respectful to people, things, and places by:
  ◦ Using kind or polite words, even when upset.
  ◦ Using manners like sharing, waiting our turn, and saying "please" and "thank you."
  ◦ Accepting differences or at least disagreeing with kind words.
  ◦ Being careful not to damage places or things.
  ◦ Using a quiet voice or calm behavior in public places.
  ◦ Sharing resources and only taking what we truly need.
• Consequences of Lacking Respect: A person who does not respect others may face:
  ◦ Exclusion from other people.
  ◦ Lack of respect from other people.
  ◦ Absence of love from others and good friendships.
  ◦ Lack of support in times of hardship.

2.7 Abiding by the Law

• Definition: Abiding by the law refers to our willingness, commitment, and responsibility to obey or follow the rules and regulations.
• Rules:
  ◦ Definition: Prescribed standards or guides for conduct in a society or organization (like a school).
  ◦ Purpose: Rules are principles that help us follow basic values (e.g., not stealing, not cheating on exams, not committing corruption, respecting fellow citizens).
  ◦ Consequences of Violation: Violating rules can lead to punishment, condemnations, and exclusion from society.
• Laws and Regulations:
  ◦ Definition: Written standards of action endorsed by authority.
  ◦ Binding Nature: They are binding, and breaking them will result in punishment.
  ◦ Examples: School rules (wearing uniform, respecting teachers, punctuality, not cheating) are considered the laws of the school. Failing to respect these rules leads to punishment.

2.8 Commitment to Excellence

• Definition: Commitment to excellence refers to one's dedication to continuously building inner strength and facing challenges, combined with taking full responsibility for behaviors, decisions, and actions to achieve great things.
• Life's Journey: Life is often challenging, full of "ups and downs" and hardships.
• Resilience: Continuous commitment to facing these challenges helps individuals become excellent and resilient citizens.
• Difficult Path: The path to excellence is not short or easy; it is challenging and often painful.
• Responsibility for Success: It involves taking full responsibility for one's endeavors in every situation to achieve great things.
• Student Example: A student demonstrates commitment to excellence by taking full responsibility for all academic endeavors to achieve excellent results.

3. The Importance of Ethical Values of Citizens

This section explains why it is critical for citizens to possess and practice ethical values, highlighting their role in personal character development and the establishment of a stable, harmonious, and prosperous nation.

3.1 Personal Significance of Ethics

• Self-Preservation: Possessing ethical values helps individuals protect themselves and future generations from various problems.
• Real Value: Ethics is a primary determinant of an individual's true worth in the world.
• Character Builder: Ethical values are fundamental in developing strong personal character.
• Essential Quality: Being ethical is an important quality that all human beings should possess, regardless of their career aspirations.
• Critical Self-Reflection: Ethics helps individuals critically examine their own lives and evaluate the morality of their actions, choices, and decisions.

3.2 Societal and National Significance of Ethics

• Peace and Harmony: Ethics guides people's behavior, leading to a peaceful and harmonious society where people live better.
• Democratic Foundation: Ethical values and behavior are critical for the existence of a democratic, stable, and prosperous society.
• Trust and Conflict Reduction: Ethical values strengthen mutual trust among citizens and reduce social conflicts.
• Societal Survival: No society can survive without a clear ethical code of conduct. Ethics is crucial even in areas where laws cannot fully reach (e.g., taking care of older parents).
• Fighting Injustice: Ethics helps people:
  ◦ Keep their words.
  ◦ Fight injustices and corruption.
  ◦ Prevent crimes and lawlessness.
  ◦ Avoid other unethical behaviors.

3.3 Benefits of a Nation with Highly Ethical Citizens

When a country has highly ethical citizens, it experiences:

• Sustainable peace
• Strong social harmony
• Rapid socio-economic transformations
• Absence of corruption
• Prevalence of the rule of law
• Justice
• Equality
• Strong national unity
• Respect for others

• Key Takeaway: Developing and practicing ethical values is not merely a choice but a necessity for citizens to overcome current crises and contribute to a nation marked by peace, justice, prosperity, and strong national unity. Ethical behavior is the bedrock of a well-functioning society.
