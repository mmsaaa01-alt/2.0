1. Problem Solving Skills[cite: 11]
1.1. Introduction to Problem Solving[cite: 11]

• Problem-solving skills are essential for everyone, enabling individuals to take control of their surroundings[cite: 11].
• Creative problem-solving: An approach that identifies unique solutions to issues through problem identification and resolution planning[cite: 11]. It's considered a soft skill[cite: 11].
• Effective problem-solving involves analyzing an issue, defining an approach, and implementing a solution[cite: 11].
• Problem-solving requires people to think critically and creatively[cite: 11].

1.2. Critical Thinking for Problem Solving[cite: 11]

• Critical thinking: The ability to engage in reflective and independent thinking[cite: 11]. It is the ability to think clearly and rationally, understanding the logical connection between ideas[cite: 11].
• Critical thinking skills enable you to analyze information and process it rationally to help you determine the value of that information or make a decision[cite: 11]. Good critical thinkers focus on facts and the scientific evaluation of the information at hand[cite: 11].
• Symbols of effective critical thinkers:[cite: 11]
  ◦ Moral integrity[cite: 11]
  ◦ Ethical consideration and action[cite: 11]
  ◦ Global citizenship practices[cite: 11]
• Important aspects of critical thinking:[cite: 11]
  ◦ Being open-minded[cite: 11]
  ◦ Critical analysis[cite: 11]
  ◦ Being fair and reasonable[cite: 11]
• Critical thinking is the process to rationally analyze and attempt to solve a problem accurately and efficiently[cite: 11].

1.3. Creative Thinking for Problem Solving[cite: 11]

• Effective critical thinkers are also largely creative thinkers[cite: 11].
• Creative thinkers think 'outside the box'[cite: 11].
• Creativity can help to think of new methods, approaches and solutions for the problem[cite: 11].
• Creative problem-solving skill: The ability and capability to move to clarify the trouble in an impressive way and at the right time[cite: 11].
• Creativity can help to effectively and refine our designs to increase efficiency[cite: 11].
• Difference between critical and creative thinkers: Creativity is associated with generating ideas, while critical thinking is associated with analyzing and evaluating those ideas[cite: 11].
• Creative problem solving involves the ability to think critically and rationally to easily overcome the problem we encountered independently[cite: 11].

2. Foreign Relations[cite: 11]
2.1. The Concept of Foreign Relations[cite: 11]

• This section defines foreign relations, foreign policy, and discusses principles of foreign policy and external relations of Ethiopia[cite: 11].
• Aristotle: "Man is a social animal"[cite: 11].
• Human beings fulfill and satisfy their basic needs in association with others because no one is self-sufficient[cite: 11].
• No state can exist or sustain in isolation from other states[cite: 11].
• A particular state must have strong relationships with other states in order to fulfill or ensure its national interests[cite: 11].
• Foreign relation: The external relations of a country with countries of the world[cite: 11].
• Ethiopia has a long history of foreign relations, mainly to protect its national interests and respect for its sovereignty[cite: 11].
• Article 86 of the 1995 FDRE constitution: States Ethiopia's principles for external relations[cite: 11].
  ◦ Based on the protection of the national interests of the country and ensuring its sovereignty[cite: 11].
  ◦ Based on mutual benefits and equality of the states[cite: 11].
  ◦ Observance of international agreements[cite: 11].
  ◦ Non-interference in the internal affairs of other states[cite: 11].
  ◦ Promoting mutual economic development and peaceful co-existence and strong public relations with its neighbors and other African states[cite: 11].

2.2. National Interest[cite: 11]

• National interest: Goals, claims, demands which states always seek to achieve, preserve, and secure in relations with other nations[cite: 11].
• Every state attempts to achieve or secure different national interests:[cite: 11]
  ◦ Political[cite: 11]
  ◦ Socio-economic[cite: 11]
  ◦ Cultural[cite: 11]
  ◦ Security interests[cite: 11]
• National interest can also be understood as survival of a state[cite: 11]. It is the protection of the sovereignty of the state from external interventions or aggressions[cite: 11].
• Securing its national interest is a universally accepted right of each state[cite: 11].
• The behavior and activities of a state are always governed by its national interests[cite: 11].
• All states devise and continuously search for national security, political independence, and territorial integrity[cite: 11].
• Maintaining or preserving both domestic and external security is among the most priority issues of the national interest of a given state[cite: 11].
• The absence of peace and security in one state can be a threat to the security of another state[cite: 11].
  ◦ Example: The security crises in Somalia has been a case of concern for Ethiopia[cite: 11].
• The achievement or success of the socio-economic development of one state depends on that of another[cite: 11].
  ◦ Example: Ethio-Djibouti relationships. Almost 90 per cent of Ethiopia's imports arrive via the port of Djibouti[cite: 11].

2.3. Foreign Policy[cite: 11]

• States can realize their foreign relations through their foreign policies[cite: 11].
• Foreign policies: The agreements, strategies, methods and guidelines that states usually use to accomplish their actions in the international arena[cite: 11].
• Foreign policy is the most important element by which states are able to convert their broadly conceived goals and interest into concrete reality[cite: 11].
• A particular state needs to formulate its foreign policies mainly to protect its national security, core values and interest, ideological goals and economic development[cite: 11].
• The main aim of foreign policy of a given state is to accomplish its national interest and maintain its national ideology[cite: 11].
• All states are subject to the influence of the decisions and actions of other nations[cite: 11].
• A state without a foreign policy is compared with a ship in the deep sea without any knowledge of directions[cite: 11].
• Foreign policy leads a state in fulfilling its national interests and acquiring rightful place in the international arena[cite: 11].

Key Takeaways: Foreign Relations[cite: 11]

• No state survives without some relationships with other states[cite: 11].
• Foreign relation is the external relations of a country with countries of the world[cite: 11].
• Ethiopia's principles for external relations are stated under Article 86 of the 1995 FDRE constitution[cite: 11].
• Ethiopia's foreign relations shall be based on the protection of the national interests of the country and ensuring its sovereignty[cite: 11].
• The main aim of foreign policy of a given state is to accomplish its national interest and maintain its national ideology[cite: 11].

3. Instruments of Foreign Policy[cite: 11]

    States employ different instruments to implement their foreign policy. Among these the major ones are diplomacy, propaganda, economic tools and military methods[cite: 11].

3.1. Diplomacy[cite: 11]

• Diplomacy: The ways states manage their sovereign relations with other mutually recognized states[cite: 11].
• It is the art and practice of conducting negotiations between nations[cite: 11].
• It is the skill of handling affairs without arousing hostility[cite: 11].
• Diplomacy is an instrument or process by which foreign policy is carried out[cite: 11].
• It is used to provide the machinery and the personnel by which foreign policy is executed[cite: 11].
• Diplomacy is not an end but a means, not a purpose but a method[cite: 11].
• It seeks, by the use of reason, conciliation and the exchange of interests, to prevent major conflicts arising between sovereign states[cite: 11].

3.2. Economic Methods[cite: 11]

• Economic development is among the core instruments of the foreign policy of states[cite: 11].
• Economically all states across the world are not at the same level or status[cite: 11].
• There are well-developed nations (e.g., USA, European states, China, Japan, India)[cite: 11].
• There are least developed nations (e.g., many African states including Ethiopia, Eritrea, Djibouti, Sudan, Kenya, South Sudan)[cite: 11].
• Since all states are economically at various level of development they depend on each other to meet their national economic interests[cite: 11].
• The economic instrument of foreign policy, thus arises from this fact[cite: 11].

3.3. Military Force[cite: 11]

• Sometimes states do not accomplish their foreign policy objectives only through diplomacy or dialogue and negotiations[cite: 11].
• The foreign relationships conducted between or among states can go beyond diplomatic deal and finally lead to conflicts[cite: 11].
• States use this instrument when their national interests are not fulfilled by peaceful methods[cite: 11].
• States use military interventions as the last resort[cite: 11].
• In order to realize foreign policy through military power, the strength of a particular state matters[cite: 11].
• States with highly advanced and latest military technologies, for instance USA, Russia, China and others have a great bargaining power over other states in the international arena[cite: 11].
• This foreign policy instrument is more of violent and follows imposition of force on other countries[cite: 11].
